Ghost Hunters of South Florida · Brand refresh · Round 1

Three directions for an embroidery-first logo

Each concept is a family of four: a full badge for jacket backs, patches and the website, a chest version, a hat mark, and a one-colour version. All of them use the same four threads, with flat fills, no gradients and no grunge texture. The small panels below are drawn at real size (3.75″ chest, 2.25″ hat) so you can judge how they read at a distance.

Concept A

Shield

This is the current badge, cleaned up. It keeps the shield, the moon, the palm, the house and the ghost rising over the top edge, so it's the closest match to the plaque and the existing shirts. The camcorder and EMF meter are gone because they can't be stitched cleanly at chest size.

Concept B · my pick

Round patch

The classic club patch. "Ghost Hunters of South Florida" runs around the ring, which lets the tagline be the largest text on the badge instead of the smallest. A round edge is the cheapest patch to make, the ring scales from hat to jacket back, and the hat mark is the same circle with the ring removed.

Concept C

Viewfinder

The ghost is framed in camera-viewfinder brackets with a record dot, which says "investigators" without drawing the equipment. It's the most minimal and modern of the three and the easiest to stitch, but it's furthest from the current badge.

What to look at

Pick a direction, or mix parts

  1. B, the round patch, is my recommendation. Embroidery is the hard constraint, and B handles it best. Its tagline is the most legible, its edge is the cheapest to make, and its hat mark comes straight out of the badge.
  2. A is the safe choice if matching the plaque and the existing merch matters more.
  3. Mixing parts is fine, for example B's ring with A's haunted house inside.

These are rough mockups. The ghost, palm and lettering will be redrawn properly once you pick a direction. The fonts here are stand-ins: Alfa Slab One for the wordmark and Oswald for the lettering.
